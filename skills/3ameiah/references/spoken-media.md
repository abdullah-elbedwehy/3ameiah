# Spoken and Timed Media

Use this playbook for video scripts, podcast scripts, voiceovers, reels, transcripts, shot lists, captions, and subtitles.

## Establish the contract

1. Identify speaker, audience, platform, target runtime, delivery speed, objective, and selected mode.
2. Identify the exact source and choose one contract:
   - Verbatim cleanup: remove noise without changing claims or voice.
   - Meaning-preserving adaptation: improve sayability while preserving meaning.
   - Source-locked rewrite: use only the exact matched source.
   - Creative script: new wording within supplied facts and brief.
3. Separate spoken lines, stage directions, timestamps, shot notes, on-screen text, captions, and metadata.
4. Inventory protected names, quotes, claims, numbers, eligibility, CTA, code, URLs, and timestamps.

## Write for the ear

- Use short breath groups, natural transitions, concrete verbs, and speakable Egyptian rhythm.
- Preserve the speaker's first-person voice and established vocabulary when source material exists.
- Build hook, context, value, and CTA only when the brief calls for them; do not impose a sales template on every script.
- Count spoken words only when estimating runtime. Directions, timestamps, and shot labels do not count.
- Treat runtime as a constraint, not permission to pad, delete facts, or invent examples.
- Keep stage directions and on-screen copy visually separate from speech.
- Use the selected mode consistently, but let named characters retain source-locked regional or personal voices.

## Keep connected assets synchronized

When the request includes a script bundle:

1. Finalize the spoken script.
2. Update shot list beats to match the final spoken meaning and sequence.
3. Update on-screen text and captions from the final spoken version.
4. Preserve timestamps/cue identifiers where fixed, or retime them deliberately when authorized.
5. Check that the CTA, claims, names, and numbers match across all assets.

For SRT/VTT and timed captions, also read [special-formats-high-stakes.md](special-formats-high-stakes.md).

## Validate the result

- Read the spoken lines aloud. Rewrite dense clusters, accidental rhyme, tongue-twisters, and translated syntax.
- Estimate runtime using the speaker's known pace when available; otherwise state the assumed pace rather than claiming exact duration.
- Compare source facts, quotes, numbers, names, and claim strength.
- Check speaker attribution, sequence, shot alignment, caption completeness, cue order, timing validity, line readability, and file format.
- Verify every factual, scarcity, eligibility, and outcome claim before recording or publishing.

## Edge handling

- When several similar transcripts exist, use the exact matching source; never borrow from a neighboring lesson or episode.
- Do not silently turn transcript cleanup into a rewrite.
- If timings are malformed, repair only when authorized and report the recovery; otherwise preserve and flag them.
- A requested joke or strong slang must not weaken a medical, legal, financial, safety, or emergency instruction.
- If the requested runtime cannot hold required facts naturally, explain the tradeoff and offer the smallest viable adjustment.
