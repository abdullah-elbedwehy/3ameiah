# Education and Explanations

Use this playbook for lessons, course material, tutorials, workbooks, exercises, answers, diagrams, summaries, and technical explanations.

## Establish the contract

1. Identify learning objective, learner level, prerequisites, format, selected mode, and desired depth.
2. Locate the exact source of truth: transcript, lesson, spec, paper, book section, codebase, or supplied notes.
3. Decide whether wording can be adapted, examples can be illustrative, or every claim must be source-locked.
4. Create a terminology ledger for official names, definitions, equations, code, commands, and recurring technical English.
5. Map lesson order, figures, exercises, answers, references, and attached assets before editing.

## Build the material

- Make the reasoning colloquial, not the facts approximate.
- Move from prior knowledge to the new idea in explicit steps.
- Use examples only when supported or clearly labelled as illustrative.
- Preserve definitions, equations, code, commands, formulas, units, and official names exactly.
- Keep exercise-answer pairs aligned and maintain figure, table, caption, footnote, and lesson references.
- Use the learner's likely vocabulary without baby talk or invented youth slang.
- In summaries, preserve important distinctions and limitations; do not compress until the central logic disappears.
- Meet page or runtime requirements with useful explanation, examples, exercises, and recap only when grounded. Never pad with repetition.

## Source-fidelity pass

1. Match each factual statement and example to the exact source or mark it illustrative.
2. Check numbers, chronology, diagram labels, formulas, names, and causal relationships.
3. Confirm no nearby lesson, transcript, or answer key leaked into this artifact.
4. Check that simplified wording did not remove a condition, negation, exception, or uncertainty.

## Comprehension pass

- Can the intended learner explain the central idea after reading?
- Are prerequisites introduced before they are used?
- Does each example demonstrate the stated concept rather than merely decorate it?
- Are technical terms kept where useful and explained in natural Egyptian Arabic?
- Are exercises answerable from the taught material, and are answers correct?

## Validate the medium

- For HTML or code, run structure and rendering checks and read [product-ui.md](product-ui.md).
- For PDF, DOCX, slides, or workbooks, read [documents-slides-pdf.md](documents-slides-pdf.md) and render the artifact.
- For spoken lessons, read [spoken-media.md](spoken-media.md) and test sayability/runtime.

## Edge handling

- If the exact source is unavailable, say so and do not synthesize a source-locked lesson from memory.
- Do not invent percentages, diagrams, research citations, learner outcomes, or course promises.
- Keep uncertainty visible. Friendly language must not create false certainty.
- If an example could be mistaken for real data, label it clearly or use supplied evidence.
